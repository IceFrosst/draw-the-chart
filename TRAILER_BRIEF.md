# Draw The Chart — Trailer Brief

## The one line

A 40-second film that makes drawing a line on a chart feel like pulling a slot lever,
without ever looking like a slot machine.

## The core direction (read this first)

The product is a dark, precise, data-dense trading interface. The feeling we want is
casino: tension, reward, dopamine, speed.

**Resolve that by splitting the job between senses.**

- **Picture stays sophisticated.** Restrained, dark, monospace, exact. No gold gradients,
  no confetti, no coin showers, no neon, no lens flares, no spinning 3D logos.
- **Sound and pacing carry the casino.** Reel-stop ticks, rising tension, a hard mechanical
  lock, a held silence, an impact on the payout.

If the picture tries to feel like a slot machine we get a cheap crypto ad. If the audio
doesn't, we get a corporate SaaS video. The whole brief is that balance.

## Beat sheet — hero cut

Timecodes are targets, not law. The draw and the reveal are the two beats that must land;
everything else can be trimmed to protect them.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Cold open. Four live tickers — AAPL, NVDA, BTC, OIL — streaming across black. Data everywhere, no explanation. | Trading floor hum. Low sub. Rapid data ticks, almost subliminal. |
| 0:03–0:05 | **Hard freeze.** Every line stops mid-flight. Three of them dissolve. | Everything cuts to near-silence. One clean impact. This contrast is the hook. |
| 0:05–0:08 | NVDA alone. The chart rescales into real price space — a controlled zoom, not a whip. | A single sustained tone rising underneath. |
| 0:08–0:10 | The anchor locks. A vertical line drops. The future zone opens to the right, empty. | Mechanical clunk. Something heavy closing. |
| 0:10–0:16 | **THE HERO SHOT.** A hand draws the path directly on the chart. Slow enough to read, fast enough to feel confident. The line glows as it forms. Cut between screen and a macro shot of the finger/stylus. | Pitch rises with the line. A held breath. This is the slot lever. |
| 0:16–0:18 | Control points snap onto the drawn line. "LOCKED." Stake and horizon stamp in. | Hard stamp. Three quick mechanical clicks as points land. |
| 0:18–0:20 | **Dead air.** The chart just sits there. Nothing moves. | Silence. Almost no sound at all. Hold longer than feels comfortable. |
| 0:20–0:26 | Reality sweeps in from the anchor — the real NVDA path, jagged, tracking the drawn line, diverging, converging. | Low rumble building. Ticking accelerates. |
| 0:26–0:32 | Score tallies. Four component bars fill in sequence. The total counts up and lands. | Reel-stop ticks per component. Four discrete mechanical stops. |
| 0:32–0:36 | Multiplier resolves. The money lands. | Impact. Sub-bass drop. One filtered chip sound — processed, expensive, not arcade. |
| 0:36–0:40 | Wordmark. One line of copy. URL. | Tail. Single sustained note decaying. |

## What the copy says

Minimal. The film should work with the sound off, but it is not an explainer.

- Around 0:10: `Draw what happens next.`
- Around 0:20: `The market answers.`
- Around 0:32: `Paid on how close you got.`
- End card: `DRAW THE CHART` / `drawthechart.xyz`

Set in DM Sans. Tight tracking. No sentence longer than five words.

## Sound design — the most important part of this brief

The casino feeling lives here almost entirely. Specific asks:

- **Reel-stop ticks** on the four score components. Four discrete mechanical stops, like a
  slot reel landing. This is the single most important sound in the film.
- **Rising pitch** through the draw, resolving on the lock.
- **A real silence** at 0:18. Not quiet — silence. It should feel like a mistake for a
  moment. Then the reveal hits harder.
- **One chip or coin sound** at the payout, heavily processed — low-passed, reverbed,
  slowed. It should read as money without reading as an arcade.
- **No** slot jingles, no cash-register ka-ching, no casino ambience, no crowd cheer.

Music: tense, pulsing, minimal. Not an EDM build-and-drop.

Reference feel: the Uncut Gems score (Daniel Lopatin) for anxiety-luxury; Sicario for
patient dread; Apple and Nike product films for precision and restraint. Send all three.

## Motion language

- Everything eases. Nothing moves linearly. Nothing bounces.
- Camera moves are push-ins only, subtle, under 5% scale.
- Numbers count with easing and land with a barely perceptible overshoot.
- Cuts land on the beat of the track.
- No spinning, no cartoon squash, no particle bursts, no confetti.
- Transitions between beats should feel like a machine advancing a state, not a slideshow.

## What we supply

- Screen recordings from the live product at 60fps, 2x pixel density, no browser chrome.
- The round seed for the featured round, so any frame can be reproduced exactly.

- Colour tokens above.

- The scored round we want featured, with its real score, multiplier and payout.

## Hard rules

1. **Every number on screen must come from a real scored round.** We can reproduce any round
   from its seed. Nothing is invented for the film. If a figure appears, we can show the
   round that produced it.
2. **No fabricated social proof.** No fake user counts, no invented testimonials, no
   made-up press logos.
3. **Show the downside once.** A frame or a line acknowledging that most rounds lose. A
   trailer that only shows wins is both misleading and a problem in most jurisdictions that
   regulate advertising for stake-based products.
4. **Carry the disclaimer.** `Illustrative. Sandbox.` legible on the end card, and on any
   frame showing a payout.
5. **No language implying guaranteed or expected returns.** No "make money", no "earn", no
   "profit" as a promise. "Get paid on accuracy" is fine. "Win $2,500" is not.
6. **No real person's likeness or a named public trader** without written release.

## The risk to design against

The product is a dark chart interface. Forty seconds of one dark chart is monotonous no
matter how well animated. The film needs at least two textures beyond the screen recording —
macro shots of the drawing gesture (hand, stylus, phone glass, reflected light) and full-frame
typography cards. Plan the intercut structure before animating anything.

## How to brief them in one paragraph

> We need a 40-second trailer for a product where you bet on the shape of a price path by
> drawing it. The feeling is casino — tension, reward, speed — but the picture has to stay
> sophisticated: dark, precise, monospace numbers, no gold or confetti. Put all the casino
> energy in the sound design and the pacing. The structure is four live markets, everything
> freezes on one, the player draws their prediction, a held silence, then reality arrives and
> the round scores with reel-stop ticks. Two shots matter: the draw and the reveal. Every
> number is real and reproducible from a round seed.
